01 · Pronouns

Choose the person.
Then choose the role.

English pronouns mostly change for subject and object. Tagalog pronouns use three practical sets based on what the participant is doing in the sentence.

The shortcut worth remembering

The focused participant uses the ang set. A non-focused actor uses the ng set. Destinations, recipients, and many other oblique roles use the sa set.

02 · Respect

Small word.
Big social signal.

Po makes a statement or question respectful. Opo is the respectful form of “yes.” Use them with elders, customers, strangers, and anyone you want to address with extra courtesy.

Placement pattern Hindi + ako + pagod.

Without po, the pronoun follows the negator.

After the first phrase

Kumain po ako.

Po is usually an enclitic: it settles after the first word or phrase of its clause.

With short pronouns

Salamat po.

Short pronouns may come before po: Sinabi ko po. You will hear both the rule and natural ordering together.

Respect is not distance

Opo, naiintindihan ko.

Po can sound warm and friendly. Context and relationship—not age alone—guide the choice.

03 · Verb focus

Keep the event.
Move the spotlight.

Focus is a grammatical alignment system—not simply emphasis. The verb form identifies the event role marked by ang; the other participants are expressed with ng or sa.

The sentence-building loop

Meaning → focus → verb form → pronoun set

Start with what you want to say. Decide which participant the clause will focus on. Choose a verb form licensed for that focus, then match each participant to its marker or pronoun set.
