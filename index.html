01 · Pronouns

Choose the person.
Then choose the role.

English pronouns mostly change for subject and object. Tagalog pronouns use three practical sets based on what the participant is doing in the sentence.

Who do you mean?

The shortcut worth remembering

The focused participant uses the ang set. A non-focused actor uses the ng set. Destinations, recipients, and many other oblique roles use the sa set.

“Ang set” and “ng set” are category names—not words to add.
Focused pronounKumain ako.not Kumain ang ako. Non-focused actorBinili ko iyon.not Binili ng ko iyon. RecipientIbigay sa akin.Here, sa is part of the phrase.

Pronouns in real sentences

One person, several grammatical jobs

Focused participant

Kumain ako.

The ang-set pronoun usually follows the predicate. Move it to the front for contrast: Ako ang kumain—“I’m the one who ate.”

Non-focused actor

Binili ko ang isda.

A short ng-set actor stays near the front. It follows a negator too: Hindi ko binili ang isda.

Possession

libro ko · Akin ang libro

Put the ng-set form after a possessed noun for “my book.” Use the sa-set base as a predicate for “The book is mine.”

Recipient or destination

Ibigay mo sa akin ang libro.

Use the sa set for “to me,” “for you,” and similar oblique roles. It generally follows the short actor pronoun.

Two pronouns together

Nakita niya ako.

The short ng-set actor normally comes before an ang-set pronoun: Hindi ko siya nakita.

Special speaker → listener form

Nakita kita.

Kita can package “I” as actor and singular “you” as the other participant: “I saw you.” It is not another form of tayo.

po

Respect can change the pronoun too. Kayo, ninyo, and sa inyo may address one person respectfully: Kayo po ba si Dr. Reyes? Context distinguishes respectful singular “you” from plural “you.”

02 · Respect

Small word.
Big social signal.

Po makes a statement or question respectful. Opo is the respectful form of “yes.” Use them with elders, customers, strangers, and anyone you want to address with extra courtesy.

Placement pattern Hindi + ako + pagod.

Without po, the pronoun follows the negator.

After the first phrase

Kumain po ako.

Po is usually an enclitic: it settles after the first word or phrase of its clause.

With short pronouns

Salamat po.

Short pronouns may come before po: Sinabi ko po. You will hear both the rule and natural ordering together.

In questions

Kumain na po ba kayo?

Question particles stay near the front too. In this common pattern, po comes before ba: “Have you eaten?”

Respect is not distance

Opo, naiintindihan ko.

Po can sound warm and friendly. Context and relationship—not age alone—guide the choice.

03 · Verb focus

Keep the event.
Move the spotlight.

Focus is a grammatical alignment system—not simply emphasis. The verb form identifies the event role marked by ang; the other participants are expressed with ng or sa.

Start here most often

Report the actor’s activity

Choose actor focus when the doer or what they did drives the message. A non-specific object commonly appears in an ng phrase.

Bumili si Ana ng isda.

Shift to the affected thing

Identify the object or result

Choose object focus when an identifiable affected object is central. The object takes ang; the actor changes to an ng form.

Binili ni Ana ang isda.

More specialized choices

Promote another participant

Location, beneficiary, and instrument focus make that role the ang-marked participant. The verb must license the matching affix.

Ipinambili ni Ana ng isda ang pera.

Beginner scope: Some grammars also describe cause or reason focus. This guide concentrates on the five roles that most clearly establish the core alignment pattern.

05 · Word order

Predicate first.
Markers keep the roles clear.

The dependable beginner pattern is predicate + participants. Tagalog word order is flexible because ang, ng, sa, and their personal or pronoun counterparts identify each role.

A · Full noun phrases

Bumili si Ana ng isda sa palengke.

Predicate → focused actor → object → location. This is a safe, neutral actor-focus order.

B · Short pronouns and po

Binili ko po ang isda.

Short pronouns and particles gather near the first word. With a negator: Hindi ko po binili ang isda.

C · Topic-first with ay

Ang isda ay binili ni Ana.

Ay allows a marked topic-first order. It is useful, but predicate-first order is the better everyday default.

1

Begin with the verb or another predicate unless you have a reason to front the topic.

2

Place short pronouns and enclitic particles such as po near the first full word.

3

Use markers—not English position—to identify the focused participant, actor, object, and location.

4

Treat alternate orders as context-sensitive choices, not random rearrangements.
